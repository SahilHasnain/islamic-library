# Second Part

In this part, responses are given to the objections raised against the epithets of the Master of the Prophets Muhammad ﷺ — "الأول والآخر والظاهر والباطن" (Al-Awwal wal-Aakhir waz-Zaahir wal-Baatin) — from the treatise *"إعلام الأذكياء" (I'lam al-Adhkiya')* by Maulana Salamatullah, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>.

---

## One Question, One Answer

*هو الأول والآخر والظاهر والباطن. الحمد لله!*

(From the preceding pages) The truth has become manifest, the correct state of affairs has become clear, the sun of guidance has shone forth without any veil. All of this is Allah's grace upon us and His favour upon others, but most people do not give thanks for it.

Whoever carefully studies the writings of this humblest of servants will surely benefit. Whoever ponders over these writings with heart and sight will find answers to all the objections of these obstinate people. But remember, clear exposition on these matters is more beneficial, so we shall try to write separate answers to these objections.

The objectors present a passage from the treatise *"إعلام الأذكياء" (I'lam al-Adhkiya')* by the eminent scholar Abuz Zaka Maulana Salamatullah, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, published in India, found on its final page. He wrote:

> *وصلى الله على من هو الأول والآخر والظاهر والباطن وهو بكل شيء عليم*

**Translation:** May Allah send blessings upon him who is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden, and he is the knower of all things.

In response to these objectors, I shall clarify that the eminent author sent this treatise to me and requested that I write an endorsement (*taqreez*) for it. I wrote an endorsement in these words:

"The statement of Zaid is true and correct; the claim of Baqar is rejected and vile. Indeed, Allah, may His majesty be exalted, granted His honoured Beloved ﷺ the knowledge of all the former and latter people. The entire world, from east to west, from the Throne to the earth, was shown to him. He was made a witness to the *Malakut* of the heavens and the earth. He was made the knower of all that was and all that will be, from the first day to the last. The eminent Mujeeb — may Allah, the Most Near, the Most Responsive, preserve him — has explained this matter with necessary detail. If anyone doubts, then the Mighty Quran is a just witness and a decisive verdict. Allah Almighty said:

**Translation:** 'And We have sent down to you the Book as a clear exposition of all things.'"

I wrote a detailed endorsement up to the end of this argument. From this writing, any ordinary person or learned scholar will reach the conclusion that I had only undertaken in my endorsement that the proofs presented by the eminent author were sufficient for the purpose, and that I had not examined every single word of the treatise. Just as the claim is mentioned separately in his text, the reality is that whoever possesses reason and discretion and has access to the assemblies of scholars knows the difference between one who writes an endorsement and one who certifies a work.

If one who writes an endorsement writes: "I have examined the treatise or the verdict from beginning to end with careful deliberation" — just as Gangohi wrote in his endorsement of *Barahine Qatia* — then he becomes responsible for the correctness or deficiency of that treatise or verdict. Similarly, the one writing an endorsement bears responsibility for all topics and statements written in the book. All the meanings and expressions of the book are considered endorsed and confirmed by the one writing the endorsement.

But if one writes: "We have examined this book at various points and consider it beneficial," and then praises and commends it, yet remains silent about its style of writing, method of expression, proofs, and fluency of words and meanings, without any affirmation or denial, and merely writes on the verdict: "The ruling is correct" — even if there are objectionable words at some points — he has only declared the ruling itself as correct. If he added the word "deficient," that opinion would bear responsibility for the book's deficiencies. Yes, if those certifying a work have also certified the proofs in their own words, then they would bear responsibility for those proofs as well.

If such an endorsement writer and certifier have, at certain points, affirmed the correctness of words and meanings, made additions or deletions, and mentioned the correction of those words, then he would have to accept responsibility for all the contents and meanings of the book.

If a certifier wrote: "What is your opinion about the extraneous and superfluous words that have no connection to any proof, nor any relationship with the claim?" — then as scholars, we would demand that the endorsement writer did not pay special attention to those superfluous matters.

## Second Answer

The same thing happened with me. I do not recall the exact word-for-word text of the original draft of this book. However, the author's Arabic translation, written in his well-known hand — the same hand in which his other treatises and verdicts come — was as follows:

> "Blessings be upon him who is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden, and the Knower of all things; upon him who is the manifestation of this noble verse — He is the First and the Last, He is the Manifest and the Hidden, and He is the Knower of all things."

No one can even imagine objecting to this. Yes, it is possible that when the work went to the press, the scribe or proofreader changed the word "manifestation" (*mazhar*) from "min." Similarly, the scribe might have written "*Majma'un*" where my endorsement had the word "Muhammad." If someone is under such a misapprehension, then fine; otherwise, we will assume that the original text is exactly as printed.

I know Mujeeb, I recognize him. He is a Sunni scholar of correct creed, of sound disposition, relentless in striking the deviant and the obstinate. He presents his brothers' words in the best possible manner to the best of his ability. I hope from my believing Sunni brothers that they will view this matter with the best interpretation and explanation. But those whose hearts have sickness will remain deprived and will fabricate all sorts of tales.

Some people have become confused about the letter "*Min*" and "*Mann*" (with *sukoon* on the *noon* and with *tashdid* on the *noon*) in this verse. They read it as an interrogative particle, not annexing it with *tashdid* to the noble verse. If read correctly, the meaning would become clear: Allah sends blessings upon those who are the bounty of this noble verse — and they are the Master of the Prophets Muhammad ﷺ. Allah Almighty said to the disbelievers: "They have changed the favour of Allah."

## The Master of the Prophets ﷺ Is a Divine Favour

Hazrat Ibn Abbas<sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>, said that what is meant by this verse is Muhammad Mustafa ﷺ. The Master of the Prophets ﷺ is the bounty of the Quran and Sunnah. At its particular place, the specific meanings of this noble verse are that the Master of the Prophets — the possessor of *Kawthar* and *Tasnim* — is the Last, but He is the First of the entire world. The Master of the Prophets ﷺ saw all of Allah's creation being created with his own eyes. He is the last in terms of being sent as a messenger. Thus, all the knowledge that Allah Almighty had revealed to His prophets, the Master of the Prophets ﷺ encompassed all of that knowledge, and it continued to manifest through his miracles. Furthermore, the manifestation of tidings of the unseen through him is also from the perfections of prophethood.

The Master of the Prophets ﷺ is Hidden (*Batin*) with respect to his noble self. He is the manifestation of Allah's Essence and His attributes. The Master of the Prophets ﷺ knows, through the informing of his Lord, everything that has happened from the first day and everything that will happen — every single thing. Allah Almighty made these five beautiful names manifest upon His Beloved and favoured the Master of the Prophets ﷺ with them, just as He favoured us by sending the Master of the Prophets ﷺ as a messenger. Therefore, he is the bounty of this noble verse.

## The Blessed Names of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ

There is no doubt that some of the names of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ derive from some of the blessed names of Allah Almighty. Our honoured father — <sup>قدس سره</sup> — wrote 67 blessed names of the Master of the Prophets in his book *Surur al-Quloob fi Dhikr al-Mahboob*. In our own work *Kitab al-Firdaws min al-Asma'il Husna*, we have added a considerable number. Furthermore, the hadith scholars who narrated those blessed names of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ — wherever Allah Almighty bestowed those names upon our Master — have provided their references. If one studies *Al-Mawahib al-Laduniyya* and its commentary by Allama Zurqani, all these blessed names are found there.

Hazrat Ibn Abbas<sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>, narrated a priceless hadith in which it is stated that Allah Almighty sent Hazrat Jibreel<sup>عليه السلام</sup>, to the service of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ. He presented himself in the most sacred presence and offered these four precious names. Then he explained the commentary and detail of each name and also stated the reason for each.

Allama Mulla Ali Qari wrote in *Sharh al-Shifa al-Sharif* that Talmasani narrated from Hazrat Ibn Abbas<sup>رضي الله تعالى عنه</sup>, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "Jibreel<sup>عليه السلام</sup> came to me and after greeting me said: 'Peace be upon you, O First! Peace be upon you, O Last! Peace be upon you, O Manifest! Peace be upon you, O Hidden!' After hearing these addresses, I said: 'These addresses are exclusive to Allah Almighty alone.' Hazrat Jibreel<sup>عليه السلام</sup> said: 'Indeed, these attributes are solely for Allah Almighty, but He has honoured you by granting you these attributes from His own, and has distinguished you with these attributes above all the prophets and messengers.'"

For you, He has designated names from His own names and described your attributes from His own attributes. He named you "*Awwal*" (First) because in terms of creation you are the first of the prophets. He named you "*Aakhir*" (Last) because in terms of time you are the last messenger of the age. Your own Ummah and the nations of the previous prophets will follow after you. He named you "*Batin*" (Hidden) because Allah Almighty inscribed your name alongside His own name with radiant light upon the forehead of the Throne. Your blessed name had been resounding upon the Divine Throne two thousand years before the creation of your noble father, Hazrat Adam<sup>عليه السلام</sup>. I have been commanded to convey blessings and salutations upon you, and I convey blessings and salutations."

Allah Almighty sent you as a messenger thousands of years after Hazrat Adam<sup>عليه السلام</sup>. You are both a bearer of glad tidings and a warner. He made you manifest and dominant over all religions of the world. Your *Shari'ah* was made dominant over all creeds of the world. You were granted excellence over all the creatures of the heavens and the earth. Only the one who sends blessings upon you will be counted among your obedient followers.

## Muhammad ﷺ and Mahmood, the Almighty

Your Lord is Mahmood (the Praiseworthy), and you are Muhammad (the Praised One). Your Lord is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden. You too are the First, the Last, the Manifest, and the Hidden. Upon hearing this, the Master of the Prophets ﷺ praised and glorified Allah, Who had favoured him above all the prophets, even to the extent of adorning him with His own names and attributes.

Sayyidi Abdul Wahhab Sharani, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, wrote in his books that the station of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ is unparalleled and without limit. In the secret, he is the comprehender; in manifestation, he is the non-manifest. He is the First, He is the Last, He is the Manifest, He is the Hidden.

We had halted this discussion at the sentence: "Allah sends blessings upon him who is the First, the Last, the Manifest, and the Hidden; Allah is the Knower and Knower of all things." Allah Almighty says:

**Translation:** "But he is the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets, and Allah is Knower of all things." (Surah al-Ahzaab)

If you argue about the realities of these divine verses, I will clarify that this can never be the case. The clear indication is that this pronoun does not refer to the Master of the Prophets ﷺ. Allah Almighty's following command further clarifies this:

**Translation:** "Indeed, We have sent you as a Messenger, a witness, a bearer of glad tidings, and a warner, so that you may believe in Allah and His Messenger, and honour him and revere him, and glorify Him morning and evening." (Surah al-Fath)

The tidings of "honour him and revere him" are directed towards the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, while the pronoun in "glorify Him morning and evening" refers to Allah Almighty. This is why reciters pause upon reaching "*tawqeeroo*" (revere him), making a *waqf* (stop), so that there is no doubt about the pronoun shifting reference — for purity belongs to Allah's Essence alone and glorification is exclusive to Him. This attribute cannot be ascribed to the Noble Prophet ﷺ; it shall be ascribed solely and exclusively to Allah Almighty.

## Fourth Answer

We concede that, according to you, the author has ascribed all the pronouns to the noble person of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ. However, we cannot pass judgment on anyone's intention and the inclination of their heart. Merely to fabricate meanings and then declare someone a disbeliever and expel the author from the fold of Islam — where is the justice in this?

It is an established fact that no Muslim — let alone a disbeliever — disputes the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ. Whoever has even ordinary familiarity with the states of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ, cannot deny his knowledge. As for the matter that the word "*kull*" (all) should not be used, I shall state in this regard that the Noble Quran has used the word "*kull*" in numerous places:

> "And Allah is the Knower of all things."

This word encompasses all concepts — the necessary, the possible, the impossible. All scholars of principles have unanimously accepted that there is no scholar who does not have some particularisation.

**Translation:** "Indeed, Allah has power over all things."

This power includes possible things, whether they exist or are non-existent. We have discussed this matter in great detail in our book *Subhan as-Subooh*. For if He had power over the necessary, He would not be God. If He had power over the impossible, then His annihilation would have to be among the impossible — if He had power over it, its annihilation would be possible, so His existence would not be necessary, and thus He would not be God.

**Translation:** "Indeed, Allah sees all things."

This statement includes only existent things, including the Divine Essence, Divine attributes, and possible things. Impossibilities and non-existents are not included, for a non-existent cannot be seen. Our honourable scholars have explained this in books of creed, especially Sayyid Abdul Ghani Nabulusi, who discussed this at great length in his work.

I shall clarify that many things appear to the eye that are not actually present — circles in a blazing flame, lines in falling raindrops, the room spinning when one's head spins. In such cases, we can say that his sight has erred, his vision has been deceived. The things appearing are a mistake of his sight. But Allah's sight is free from any error or deception.

**Translation:** "Allah is the Creator of all things."

This includes those possible things that have existence at some time. It does not include the necessary or the impossible, nor even that possible which never was and never will be till eternity.

**Translation:** "And all things We have enumerated in a clear record."

This verse includes only those things that have happened and will happen from pre-eternity to post-eternity. It does not include infinite knowledge, for the finite is limited, confined within boundaries, and cannot encompass the infinite. In the foregoing proofs, we have presented five verses of the Quran. In all five places, the same word "*kull*" has been used. The word is the same; in every place, generality (*umoom*) is intended. But each statement encompasses those things that are within its scope and not those outside it, and it does not have the capacity for them. No intelligent and wise person can doubt this — let alone a learned scholar and knower of the Quran!

## Fifth Answer

We have already established in the preceding pages that the verses of the Mighty Quran and the authentic hadith affirm the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ. The knowledge of all that was and all that will be — from the first day to the last, all the writings of the Preserved Tablet — is possessed by our Noble Prophet ﷺ, and the honourable scholars have also elucidated this matter.

Hazrat Allama Alauddin al-Muhaqqiq, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, wrote *Ad-Durr al-Mukhtar* and clarified that it is permissible to speak of certain names shared between the Creator and creation, with one condition kept in view: for creation, different meanings will be taken, and for Allah's Essence, different meanings will apply. When the statement that He is the Knower of all things is attributed to Allah Almighty, the first meanings — essential and universal — will be intended. But when the same statement is attributed to the Noble Prophet ﷺ, conferred and bestowed meanings will be intended. There is no impropriety in such a perspective.

Our Master, the verifying Shaykh, Abdul Haq Muhaddith Bukhari Dehlawi — <sup>قدس سره</sup> — who is counted among all the scholars and senior scholars of the Hanafis, whose scientific fame has filled the ears of the world, whose fragrance of knowledge has perfumed the cities and plains of the Islamic world — even our scholars of Makkah are aware of his grandeur and scholarly eminence. The precious works of Hazrat Shaykh Muhaddith have received praise and acclaim across the Islamic world. People have derived immense benefit from these books in religious matters. Among them are: *Ash'at al-Lam'at* (commentary on Mishkat, 4 volumes), *Jazb al-Quloob*, *Sharh Safar as-Sa'adah* (2 volumes), *Fath al-Mannan*, *Sharh Futuh al-Ghaib*, *Madarij an-Nubuwwah* (2 volumes on the states of the Noble Prophet ﷺ), *Akhbar al-Akhyar*, *Adab as-Saliheen*, and a concise treatise on the principles of hadith — all of which are studied by scholars. Nearly 300 years have passed since the demise of Hazrat Shaykh. His shrine in Delhi is a place of pilgrimage for the masses, and people receive spiritual blessings there.

This imam of great status and eminence began his book *Madarij an-Nubuwwah* with this verse, stating that just as these words express the praise and glorification of Allah — as Allah has expressed His own praise in the Quran — similarly, He has remembered His Honoured Beloved ﷺ with beloved and desired names. In the Wise Quran and sacred hadith, thousands of beautiful names that Allah Almighty has attributed to His Beloved ﷺ — such as Noor, Haqq, Haleem, Hakeem, Momin, Muhaymin, Wali, Haadi, Ra'uf, Raheem — besides thousands of shared names have been mentioned. But these four names were conferred as a special distinction upon the Master of the Prophets: Awwal, Aakhir, Zaahir, and Batin — these are likewise beautiful names. The reason and explanation for each name were also given, and the Master of the Prophets ﷺ was declared the Knower of all things.

## The Master of the Prophets' Loftiness of Knowledge

The Noble Prophet ﷺ has been favoured by Allah Almighty with many stations and ranks, including the knowledge of all things — the rulings of the attributes of Truth, the names, acts, and effects. The Master of the Prophets ﷺ has encompassed all the knowledge of the former and latter, the manifest and the hidden. He is the perfect embodiment of this noble verse: "And above every possessor of knowledge is the One All-Knowing."

*عليه من الصلوات أفضلها ومن التحيات أكملها* — May the most excellent of blessings and most complete of salutations be upon him.

If expressing these noble attributes of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ is a crime in the Shari'ah, then I would say that the sin of Hazrat Muhaddith Dehlawi would be greater than that of our Mujeeb — even though the eminent Mujeeb considers him his imam and his leader. Would you, sirs, dare to pass a verdict against Hazrat Muhaddith as well? God forbid, would he also fall within the scope of your edict and be called a disbeliever? Would he be called misguided? Is he not the great scholar, the pillar of the faith, and the inheritor of the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ? If no answer is given to this, we will remove the veils from the faces of these masked ones.

Bear in mind that in every age, the deputies (*khulafa'*) of Allah have been appointed. This chain continued until the Master of the Prophets ﷺ appeared in the world from the realm of the unseen. The last caliph of Allah Almighty was the Honoured Master of the Prophets, the Embodied Light ﷺ. He manifested upon this universe like a radiant sun.

Every light paled before his light. Every radiance was lost in his illuminations. Every command became ineffective before his command. He is the one who brought the knowledge of all things. This noble verse from Surah al-Hadid stands as a witness to his knowledge. Iron (*Hadid*) has hardness and also benefit for people. The Master of the Prophets ﷺ was sent — he was a sword, and when he overspread the world, he was a rain of mercy.

## Ayat al-Kursi Is a Eulogy of the Master of the Prophets

Allama Nizamuddin Naishapuri, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, wrote in his commentary *Ghara'ib al-Bayan wa Ghayat al-Furqan*, while explaining Ayat al-Kursi, that "*يعلم ما بين أيديهم*" (He knows what is before them) is presented as evidence of the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets. He attributed "*من ذا الذي يشفع عنده إلا بإذنه*" (Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission) to the Master of the Prophets ﷺ. He says: Who will intercede before Allah Almighty on the Day of Judgement? Only the servant of Allah, the Beloved of Allah, who has been granted permission for intercession. According to this promise of Allah Almighty, this glad tiding will be fulfilled for him when he graces the Praiseworthy Station (*Maqam Mahmood*).

"*يعلم*" — Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ knows all the states that are before him — matters before the beginning and before the creation of creation. "*ما خلفهم*" — the conditions that will manifest after him until the Day of Judgement — he knows, through his God-given knowledge, the joys, affairs, and narratives of people. He will relate the conditions and tidings of all the noble prophets. He is the one who knows all the matters of the Hereafter. The states of Paradise and Hell are before him. Ordinary people know nothing of these conditions, except that which He wills — "*إلا بما يشاء*" — as much as the Noble Prophet ﷺ wills, the knowledge and states that reach the Ummah through his grace. "*وسع كرسيه السماوات والأرض*" — his throne is vast. The heavens and the earth, the Throne and the Footstool, with all their vastness, are like a ring thrown beneath the sky. "*ولا يؤوده حفظهما*" — "The preservation of them does not weary Him." The preservation of the secrets of the heavens and the earth is not burdensome for the human spirit. Allah taught Hazrat Adam<sup>عليه السلام</sup>, all the names.

Now we ask those who object: Is this commentary and explanation of Hazrat Allama Naishapuri also heretical? (God forbid.)

## My Impressions

When I read this passage in the commentary of Naishapuri — <sup>قدس سره</sup> — it occurred to my heart that the meaning of his elucidation is that Allah Almighty, in His words "*من ذا الذي يشفع عنده إلا بإذنه*", has indicated the noble person of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ. He alone has been granted permission for intercession. He alone has been favoured with the authority of the door of intercession. He will open the door of intercession. No other is worthy of "*بإذنه*" except him.

The questioner finds no difficulty in understanding that, for the intercessor in the Divine Court, there is no alternative except that he first be fully acquainted with the state of the person whose intercession is intended. Whomever he is to intercede for, he will have complete knowledge of their ranks of faith and their inward and outward deeds. Only the one worthy of the Prophet's intercession will receive his intercession. Moreover, the Master of the Prophets will also know who requires intercession for a lapse, what kind of intercession is necessary for him, to what extent he deserves intercession, and what kind of intercession he is entitled to, and which intercession can become the cause of his salvation in the Divine Court. For there are thousands of types of intercession, and they have different occasions and stations. If the intercessor does not have knowledge, how can he intercede?

**Translation:** "They shall not speak except him whom the Most Gracious has permitted, and who speaks what is correct." (Surah an-Naba')

This permission has been granted only to Hazrat Muhammad ﷺ.

## He Encompasses the Knowledge of All Worlds

The Noble Prophet ﷺ alone encompasses the knowledge of all worlds. He alone, without doubt, knows all the worlds. He alone recognizes every thing in every moment. "*يعلم ما بين أيديهم*" — he knows all the things that are before him. "*ما كان*" — what was before him; "*ما يكون*" — what will be until the end of time. All of this knowledge was given to him by the Lord Who bestows knowledge. We have already established in the preceding pages, through prophetic hadith, that Allah Almighty has made everything clear to him — whatever was necessary to clarify — just as Allah had made things clear to His previous noble prophets.

The Master of the Prophets ﷺ came to know everything; he came to know through Allah's grant, and the Master of the Prophets ﷺ attained that comprehension of all things which others did not attain. "*ولا يحيطون بشيء من علمه*" — they do not attain anything of His knowledge unless He teaches them, and only to the extent that He wills.

He ﷺ is the sun of eminence; all others are stars that continue to spread their lights upon people, but all this light is obtained only from the fountain of light of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ.

## The Expanses of the Intercession of the Master of the Prophets

The numbers of the former and latter among those for whom the Master of the Prophets ﷺ will intercede are so vast that they cannot be counted or enumerated. He alone is the intercessor for all of them — a single personality. His heart is never constrained, nor does he tire of interceding. How can the heart of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ be constrained? He has received the glad tiding of "*وسع كرسيه السماوات والأرض*" — before him even the vastness of the heavens and the earth are negligible. Can the most wretched of wretched people imagine that the heart of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ, before which the vastness of the Throne is as limited as a mosquito before the heavens, cannot be the intercessor of the multitude of the gathering?

We may feel that perhaps, among such a vast creation, someone might forget him. But the Quran gives this answer: How can this be, when the preservation of these two heavens and the earth and the supervision of all the creation dwelling between them is his responsibility? Moreover, Allah Almighty has granted him the power of intercession through His grace. No one except Allah can gauge the expanse of his intercession.

The foregoing discussion has been presented in summary from the book *Izalat al-Awhaam*. We hope that every person connected to the cloak of knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ will be pleased with this writing and obtain heartily and intellectual delight.

## Clarification

I consider it necessary to clarify at this point that I do not fully agree with these meanings and interpretations of these verses, nor did the eminent commentator — <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> — claim this. But this discussion is based on those beautiful interpretations and allusions that the people of truth present to the people of falsehood. There is a hadith:

> "Angels do not enter a house wherein there is a dog."

The reason is that the heart is the abode of angels and divine manifestations, while the dog represents the place of lusts and impurities. The people of insight have never denied these meanings and interpretations. Indeed, the people of the inward, based on the perfection of their faith and recognition, prefer such meanings.

Allama Saduddin Taftazani, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, has occasionally stated such points in *Sharh al-Aqa'id*. Although they may be rare and unfamiliar, they are subtle. The externalist scholars criticize them and point out their errors, but like a cucumber with its blossom, they are accepted in that form. One thing leads to another spontaneously. The heart is a station that accepts advice. Sometimes, the depths of the heart derive delight and lesson — through a tone of amorous imagination like that of Laila and Salma — from the praise of the True Beloved, especially the Master of the Prophets.

It has been written in commentaries:

**Translation:** "Worship Allah as if you see Him; if you cannot achieve this, then feel that He sees you."

Some insightful ones of this Ummah paused at "*رتاہ*" (you see Him). They thought: "If you cannot do this" — that is, if you become annihilated from your self, then you will see Him. You will then reach the station of witness, for your self is your veil, due to which you remain deprived of witnessing the Truth.

Allama Ibn Hajar Asqalani objected at this point, saying: If the interpretation and meanings of these great religious figures are considered correct, then "*رتاہ*" would not have an *alif*, and it would have been "*فإنه يراك*" (for indeed He sees you), because there would be no connection with what precedes it. Then he brought successive narrations of the hadith's wording that do not support this interpretation — that if you do not see Him, then He sees you.

Hazrat Muhaddith Dehlawi, Allama Abdul Haq — <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> — answered this discussion in *Lam'at* as follows, and on this basis, in the narration of Ibn Kathir, the divine statement "*أرسله معنا غداً يرتع ويلعب*" (Send him with us tomorrow to enjoy himself and play) and the divine statement "*ومن يتق ويصبر*" (And whoever fears Allah and is patient) are also similar. A poet's verse also expresses this matter.

Grammarians know that when a conditional clause is in the past tense, *jazm* and *jarr* are not obligatory, even if the meaning is as it is here. "*فإنه يراك*" indicates the possibility of vision, and the possibility of seeing Allah is also established — that is, we can see Allah Almighty without direction, place, emergence from direction, etc. But Arab scholars have viewed these meanings from different angles; this is a matter that becomes apparent to their inner senses. This state is a condition of the overwhelming of a state, of effacement and annihilation, that descends upon their hearts.

We may summarise this discussion briefly. Just as Allama Mulla Ali Qari — <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> — refuted in his famous book *Miqat*, he discussed in detail in the first and third sections and did not argue about his second answer. He only did this: what was said is not in accordance with it. "*فإنه يراك*" relates to the preceding discourse, although its relation to the following is also possible. I cannot agree with some commentators on this point.

In some narrations, it is: "If you do not see Him, then He sees you." In my view, several other aspects of connection also present themselves. "*فإنه يراك*" — I hope this point is more subtle and precious. Just as we say "*فإن لم تكن*" — if you are not and become annihilated, desiring His witness, "*رتاہ*" — you will see Him and attain your goal. "*فإنه يراك*" — indeed, He sees you and is not unaware of you for a single moment. So when He has seen you, you have annihilated your self for Him. He does not disappoint anyone, for you have reached the station of excellence (*ihsan*), and Allah Almighty does not waste the reward of those who do good.

Present another point thus: "*فإن لم تكن*" — if you are not, then indeed you are seeing Him, for you have become annihilated and only He remains. Now He alone is the one beholding His own essence. And how would He not see, for He sees you, and you have certainly been annihilated. When you become annihilated, only then can you see Him. In Sahih al-Bukhari, it is stated that his eye has no veil. "*فإنه يراك*" — so indeed He sees you, and you are, through a form appearing in an imaginary dream, from the manifestation of reflection and shadow — how then could you not see the True Beauty and the Real Countenance?

Imam Qushayri<sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> wrote in his treatise on the authority of Hazrat Yahya bin Razi Ali that Hazrat Abu Suleman Dimashqi, while circumambulating the Ka'bah, heard the call of "Saatar Bari" and fell unconscious. When he regained consciousness, his friends inquired, and he informed them that he realized a voice was coming. "Saatar Bari" means "Coverer of the Creator" — that is, goodness and grace — although the circumambulators read it with *fatha* on the *ba'*. The eminent author Abdul Khaliq bin Muhammad bin Ahmad bin Abdul Qadir (the grandson of Sayyid Muhammad Sharqi) wrote that a man in the streets of Egypt, carrying a basket on his head, would call out "Saatar Bari". Three servants of Allah heard this call and derived different meanings from it.

One who was among the people of guidance understood "Saatar Bari" to mean: strive and continue in My obedience; the gifts of My generosity will be shown to you. The second, a moderate person, understood that this person is saying "Saatar Bari", meaning: My goodness is so vast that whoever loves Me and obeys Me will find Me. The third, from the people of the ultimate, understood that he was saying "*as-Sa'atu yarti yarbi*". All three saints entered a state of ecstasy from this single sentence, according to their own taste.

In *Ihya al-Ulum*, Hazrat Imam Ghazali wrote that sometimes even a non-Arab is moved by an Arabic couplet and experiences ecstasy, because some Arabic letters have a non-Arabic style and meter, even if the meaning is different. The non-Arab delights merely in the melodious sound. Consider this couplet:

> *ما زارني في النوم إلا خياله، فقلت له أهلاً وسهلاً ومرحبا*
> "I saw him in my sleep only as an imagined form, so I said to him: welcome and greetings."

Upon this couplet, a person unfamiliar with Arabic fell into ecstasy. People asked him: What did you understand? He replied: It means that I am near the death of the scholar of the world. He imagined that we have all reached the brink of death, and so he clothed it in his own meanings and derived the sense.

We are not explaining the noble verse in the foregoing pages; rather, we are clarifying that commentators and exegetes occasionally state various meanings according to their own perspective. In reality, we wish to make it clear that those who are veiled from and unaware of the essence of Mustafa ﷺ do not have even the familiarity with his knowledge and person that an ordinary literate person possesses — so how can they understand the station of Mustafa?

The externalist scholars have their place, but how can such externalist scholars comprehend the secrets that are granted to the saints from Allah Almighty? These people do not fear declaring Muslims disbelievers, and in their ignorance they continue to deny their faith, and then persist in this denial. Allah Almighty does not guide such people to the path of guidance. What knowledge can those deprived of Allah's light possess? They cannot attain the light of insight.

*نسأل الله العفو والعافية.* (We ask Allah for pardon and well-being.)

## Second Question

Mujeeb's statement that the Master of the Prophets Muhammad ﷺ knows everything that has happened and will happen from pre-eternity to post-eternity is astonishing and objectionable to the opponents. In my opinion, when translating Mujeeb's words, the error of the subject has been employed. In their view, the Master of the Prophets has no connection with pre-eternity (*azal*). When the term *azal* is examined in light of the science of *kalam* (scholastic theology), these would be the meanings: that the Noble Prophet ﷺ has existed from pre-eternity, which has no beginning — and this is clear disbelief, because it would entail that the Noble Prophet, the Possessor of Kawthar and Tasneem ﷺ, is eternal (*qadeem*). However, Mujeeb's statements do not say this. His text is: "Indeed, the entirety of '*ما لم تكن تعلم*' — what you did not know is included — all those unseen things that have passed from pre-eternity and that will happen until post-eternity."

---

## A Glance at "From Pre-Eternity to Post-Eternity"

In this text, the eternality or lack of beginning of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ is not established. Rather, it is established that the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ encompasses the entire universe from pre-eternity to post-eternity. It is about this that we will speak. Bear in mind that when the words "from pre-eternity to post-eternity" are uttered, the speakers intend that thing whose beginning of existence is unknown and whose end of continuance is not known. In this sense, possessing knowledge of all things is not impossible, and we have already clarified this in the preceding pages. Allah Almighty alone is the possessor of such knowledge. No creature or servant can attain this power, and affirming such knowledge for any servant is impossible according to reason and revelation. However, many times, those who say "post-eternity" and "pre-eternity" merely mean a long duration of the past and the future.

---

## The Meanings of *Abad* (Post-Eternity)

Hazrat Qazi Baidawi, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, has explained the meanings of *abad* as follows:

> "*Qidm* (pre-eternity) is that which has no beginning."

This meaning is also applied, metaphorically, to that whose life is long.

Similarly, the gnostic of Allah, Imam Allama Sayyidi Abdul Wahhab Sharani, wrote in his book *Jawahir ad-Durar*, citing his Shaykh, the gnostic of Allah, Sayyid Ali Khawwas<sup>قدس سره</sup>: I asked my teacher: "What is meant when we say that Allah has written in pre-eternity, even though pre-eternity has no relation — it is merely a time, and time is created, while Allah's writing is eternal?" He replied: "What is meant by the pre-eternal writing is only the divine knowledge, which encompasses all things. But *azal* is the time that is conceived between the existence of Allah Almighty and those existents. In this time, Allah Almighty took the covenant (*meesaaq*) from all of humanity to acknowledge Him as their Lord, and this is the time when the noble prophets made the pledge to fulfil the covenant."

From this discussion, it is clear that the questioner did not take *azal* in the sense of time, but rather it is something created, originated, and not eternal. The gnostic of Allah, Mujeeb, has clarified this point: *azal* is the time when Allah Almighty took the covenant. Now no doubt remains about the meanings of *azal*.

Imam Ahmad bin Khateeb Qastalani, <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup>, stated in *Mawahib Laduniyya* that Allama Abu Muhammad Mushaqqar Suqrati — <sup>رحمة الله تعالى عليه</sup> — said beautifully in his famous *qasida*: All dominions belong to Allah. This honour of prophethood was granted on the day of pre-eternity. If by *azal* is meant pre-eternity, where was the Throne at that time?

My master, the gnostic of Allah, Hazrat Maulana Nizami Ganjwi — <sup>قدس سره</sup> — said a eulogistic couplet in the court of the Noble Prophet ﷺ:

> *Azal se abad tak jo cheez bhi paida ki gai*
> *Wo to Huzoor ﷺ ke isme girami se hi zaahir hui thi*

"Whatever was created from pre-eternity to post-eternity, it all manifested only through the blessed name of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ."

That is, all things are from the servants and retinue of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ, and all these splendours are for his honour and dignity. I ask the objectors: What did Hazrat Nizami mean by *azal* here? If it is taken in the terminological sense of *kalam*, then God forbid, it is clear disbelief. Therefore, it must be interpreted according to the words of the Master, the Recognizer of Allah. In my view, these are the correct and sound meanings. Instead of "from pre-eternity to post-eternity," it should be read as "from the first day to the Day of Judgement." But it is the habit of objectors that they just keep objecting.

## Second Answer

If one were to place before oneself the text from page 16 of the original book and read it carefully, the meaning of Mujeeb's text would have become perfectly clear, and they would have arrived at the correct meaning, just as we have. He states that in the Preserved Tablet, all past things are part of the Preserved Tablet. In other words, these people have considered the time of the first and the last as limited within the Preserved Tablet. If all finite knowledge is not even the adornment of the Preserved Tablet, then what hesitation do they have in accepting the knowledge and names from the day of pre-eternity to post-eternity?

In an authentic hadith, the Noble Prophet ﷺ said that all things until post-eternity are present and inscribed in the Tablet. What is meant by "present and inscribed" in it is exactly what we are saying.

## Third Answer

If only these gentlemen would read page 11 of the original book! From *Tafsir Ruh al-Bayan*, this passage is quoted:

> "O Prophet! You are not hidden or veiled from the grace of your Lord. Whatever happened from pre-eternity and whatever will happen until post-eternity has not been kept hidden from you — the word that implies concealment. Nay, you are informed of everything that has passed and is to come."

This eminent commentator has clearly explained the meanings of our Allama Mujeeb. If this is a sin, let the objection be made to the author of *Tafsir Ruh al-Bayan*, who, in his capacity as a leading authority, is commenting on the Quran. For Mujeeb merely clarified, in his own words, the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets, whereas the eminent commentator, while explaining the very words of Allah Almighty, is stating the same knowledge of Mustafa. So why is the verdict of disbelief and misguidance not passed against the commentator? First, pass a verdict against the author of *Tafsir Ruh al-Bayan*, then make Mujeeb the target of criticism.

## Third Question

Mujeeb's claim that the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ encompasses all hidden things has also not pleased the objectors. If by "all" is meant the comprehensive encompassment, in detail, of all divine knowledge and lordly information, then we have already written earlier that such a perspective is impossible for any creature, both rationally and according to Shariah. However, if it is said that the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ, encompasses everything that happened from pre-eternity and will happen until post-eternity, then there is no doubt or ambiguity in this. The knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ encompasses all affairs of pre-eternity and post-eternity; this is the truth and is correct. This is established by the words of Allah and His Messenger. If only these people would reflect! When Allah Almighty says: "*تبياناً لكل شيء*" (a clear exposition of all things), the Master of the Prophets ﷺ said: "Everything was made clear to me."

The view of the honourable scholars is that the Master of the Prophets ﷺ had attained all particular and universal knowledge, and that he had encompassed all the knowledge of the universe. So what room remains to doubt the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ? When the Noble Prophet ﷺ explained everything, the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ encompassed the entire universe. The Noble Prophet ﷺ knew all that had passed and all that will be. The Master of the Prophets ﷺ sees the events of the entire universe as if they are happening before his eyes. He is the knower of all created things. He has encompassed all knowledge — the first and the last, the manifest and the hidden. Moreover, it is also a reality that everything becomes manifest to the gnostic.

Now, after these statements and texts, what obstacle remains in accepting the encompassment of all hidden things? Do people consider their own statements and intellect more important than the words of Allah, the words of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the noble sayings, and the pronouncements of the honourable scholars?

If our objectors would but use their intelligence, the more they ponder the expanses of knowledge, the more the expanses of the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ would open up to them. If this doctrine is disbelief, ignorance, or folly, then first change the words of Allah and His Messenger, declare the scholars and imams as disbelievers and misguided, and then issue edicts against Allama Mujeeb.

## Fourth Question

The objectors also raise this objection: Does the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ have a beginning or an end? Is there any limit or count to this knowledge? I say: A beginning certainly exists, because he is a created being, and the knowledge of a created being is originated (*hadith*) — and it is indeed originated. But bear in mind this point: the counting and enumeration of the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ is only in the knowledge of Allah Almighty. No one else can enumerate or count it. No human or angel has the capacity to count the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ. It is also incorrect that the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets stops or halts at any point or that its progress is obstructed. Allah Almighty has made the knowledge of His Beloved continuously increasing. Our Master will continue to progress in the knowledge of the essence and attributes of Allah till endless eternity. We have already written in detail about this subject in the preceding pages.

## Fifth Question

The objectors say: When I say that not even an atom's weight is missing from the knowledge of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, what is meant? Does it mean that nothing is missing from the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets from pre-eternity to post-eternity, or is something else meant? I say: If even a single particle were excluded from the knowledge of the Master of the Prophets ﷺ, it would clearly point towards origination (*huduth*). Instead of "atom" (*dharrah*), the word "*mithqal*" (weight) was added, creating confusion in the question — although I had not used the word "*mithqal*." The objector himself added some things to my words to pave the way for objection and hesitation. For in this way, by presenting the word "*mithqal dharrah*," the objector attempts to prove that the particle existed at that time between pre-eternity and post-eternity and wants to have the existence of particles from pre-eternity accepted. This is a misleading notion.

He added the word "*mithqal*" to make it believed that even in pre-eternity, something was being weighed — although in pre-eternity there is no such thing that could be weighed in scales. There, only the essence of Allah Almighty exists, His perfect attributes. The hesitation and possibility remained inclined towards disbelief or manifested therein. This is actually the foundation of these misleading doctrines. This action is such that digs pits for others but falls into them itself.

## Three Categories of People

We have repeated this point time and again and have made it as clear as the bright day: the word "pre-eternity" (*azal*) is neither in my words, nor are the meanings and sense that the objector wants to take from it, nor is it my intention. I have repeated this three times in the second answer. People have three ranks:

The first rank is that of a righteous Muslim who lives his life in peace and does not harbour ill feelings towards other Muslims. If, unintentionally, he hears such a word that can carry two meanings, he interprets it favourably and turns it away from evil and harm.

The second is that rank where one does not have such grace, but protects himself, through his piety, from such an interpretation that would cause discord. Such a person safeguards his faith and does not think of meaningless things for his brothers that would bring any kind of disgrace and accusation.

The third is that person who is entirely deprived of the above-mentioned graces and reaches the ultimate limit, but still has some modesty left in his eyes. Even if he perceives something evil, he does not dare to disclose it, because the modesty of his eye prevents him from slander, and he does not cause hurt to others with his tongue. Yes, in society, there are also some individuals who are victims of envy; they are destroyed and cross all limits. They see but turn away from the truth. Hearing an ordinary thing, they keep objecting.

I warn such attacking gentlemen that they will always remain in loss. It is better for them to benefit from my stated issues and requests. Let them not twist and distort my words to give them meanings that could never even be imagined. A great elucidation of the word *azal* exists. My intention is only that elucidation and interpretation. The meanings of "the Last" and "the first day" are clear; moreover, the doors of interpretation and elucidation are not closed. But the disease of envy destroys and ruins a person.

O my friends! Avoid all these defects. Allah Almighty is responsible for our and your guidance.
